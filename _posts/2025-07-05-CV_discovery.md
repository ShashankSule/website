Last month marked the culmination of a two major projects regarding collective variable discovery, a fundamental interdisciplinary problem
in drug discovery, computational statistical physics, and stochastic processes. From a probabilistic perpsective, this problem asks: how can 
we map a stochastic process to low dimensions and still preserve its statistics? In two case study-style papers on the
[butane molecule](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01222) and [Lennard-Jones clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00408) we provide some answers 
by resorting to quantitive coarse graining theory and proposing algorithms that use some of my favourite tools from geometric data science.
